# `contrib/` — vendor-contributed entries, before ratification

Entries here were generated by [`nsjepa-contrib`](../sdk/README.md) from running
deployments and submitted by pull request. They are **not** part of the
canonical dictionary. That is the point of the directory.

```
contrib/<vendor_id>/<deployment_id>/<lemma_slug>.entry.yaml
```

## Rules for this directory

1. **Everything here is `status: provisional`.** Validation errors on a
   contributed entry that claims otherwise. A contributor cannot ratify its own
   entry, and only ratified entries may feed institution-level aggregation.
2. **Nothing here is loaded by `core/` as domain content.** A contribution is
   evidence attached to a lemma, not a definition of one. Each carries
   `instance_of: <domain_prefix>:<lemma_slug>` naming the lemma it attaches to.
3. **Promotion is a maintainer action** and is a normative decision, not a
   merge. It means moving the evidence into the hand-maintained entry under
   `domains/<domain>/entries/`, with the tier and confidence a human has
   accepted — not moving the file.

## Reviewing a contribution

Read `term_findings` before the causal edges.

Where a governance term the solicitation required was found NOT met in
production, the SDK has **withdrawn** the corresponding warrant rather than
asserting it, and the entry may therefore contradict the contract it was
produced under. That is working as intended and is the most informative thing
in the file.

Then check, in this order:

- **Tier discipline.** Is a positive polarity resting on a `vendor:` or
  `runtime:` edge alone? The builder blocks this, but an entry can arrive from
  a fork with the gate patched out. `nsjepa-contrib validate` re-runs the check
  on the receiving side; run it rather than trusting that it ran.
- **Node names.** Unregistered nodes are warnings. Each one is either a genuine
  addition worth registering in `domains/<domain>/vocab/nodes.yaml`, or a fork
  of a node that already exists, and the second is much more common. A silent
  fork does not fail anything — it just quietly ends the convergence property.
- **`warrants: []`.** Escalation, not omission. It means no rule in the domain
  reaches the harm, and it needs a human normative decision before the entry
  can be ratified.
- **Sample sizes.** `n` on a runtime finding is the number of observed events,
  not the number of people affected, and the confidence attached to it comes
  from a placeholder shrinkage function rather than a real interval.

## What a contribution is not

It is not an audit, and it should not be cited as one. Runtime telemetry
establishes what a vendor's instrumentation reported. Converting that into a
finding about what actually happened requires the independent `audit:` tier,
which by construction cannot be produced by the party being audited.
